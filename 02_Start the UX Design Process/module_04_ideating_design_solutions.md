## 1. Presentation Formats: Written Reports vs. Slideshows

| Format | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Written Reports** | Concise, efficient, and less time-consuming to create; best when communicating insights quickly to busy stakeholders |
| **Slideshow Presentations** | Visually engaging and dynamic using software like Google Slides, Microsoft PowerPoint, or Apple Keynote; require more time and practice to prepare |

- **Decision Basis**: Format depends on audience availability, client expectations, and required depth of detail.

---

## 2. Structure & Best Practices for Slideshow Presentations

- **Introduction Slide**: Clearly state audit goals, research questions, methodology, and compared aspects.
- **Section Headers**: Use bold title slides with simple, solid visuals to signal new topic sections.
- **Product Summaries**: Compare competitor products against yours, highlighting both advantages and areas needing improvement.
- **Clean Visual Layouts**: Avoid cluttered screens; keep layouts clean so the audience can focus on primary data.
- **Conclusion Slide**: End with a summary recapping key takeaways, as reviewing up to 10 companies provides a large volume of information.

## 3. Presenting Data Effectively

| Method | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Charts & Graphs** | Convert numbers and quantitative data into charts or graphs to clearly illustrate comparative metrics |
| **Screen Recordings** | Include recordings to demonstrate functional issues or specific competitor features in real time |
| **Grading Rubrics** | Provide clear evaluation criteria (e.g., rating performance on a scale from 1 to 4, ranging from Needs Work to Outstanding) |

## 4. Delivery & Speaker Best Practices

- **Team Feedback**: Share slides with teammates prior to presenting to ensure optimal flow.
- **Text Minimalist**: Limit slide text to main focal points and save detailed context for spoken delivery.
- **Focus on Highlights**: Keep main slides focused on top takeaways; move detailed breakdowns to an appendix or written report.
- **Speaker Notes**: Use outlines or note cards to keep timing on track.
- **Practice**: Run trial rehearsals ahead of time to polish pacing and content familiarity.
- **Objective Evidence**: Back up conclusions using specific user data while actively avoiding personal design biases.

## 5. Competitive Audit Written Report Framework

When compiling findings into an executive report, summarize data across these 8 core sections:

| # | Section | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Audit Goals** | Specific statement detailing the precise focus (e.g., comparing purchasing experiences for new vs. returning users) |
| 2 | **Key Competitors** | Direct and indirect competitor profiles summarized in 1–2 sentences |
| 3 | **Product Quality** | Brief paragraphs (2–5 sentences) detailing competitor product offerings and performance |
| 4 | **Market Positioning** | Target audience characteristics (age, income, habits) |
| 5 | **Brand Messaging** | Unique value propositions summarized in 2–3 sentences |
| 6 | **Strengths & Weaknesses** | 2–4 bullet points detailing top successes and areas for improvement for each competitor |
| 7 | **Market Gaps** | 2–3 unaddressed market needs, missing features, or accessibility gaps |
| 8 | **Opportunities** | Actionable design decisions that enable your product to stand out in the market |
