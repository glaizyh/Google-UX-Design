## 1. Overview & History of UX Design

- **Course Purpose**: Accessible to anyone without prior design experience or a college degree.
- **Role of UX Designers**: Help make technology easier to understand and more enjoyable to use.
- **Origin of the Term**: Coined in the 1990s by cognitive psychologist **Don Norman** as technology usage expanded.
- **Historical Origins**: Principles trace back thousands of years to ancient China and the practice of **Feng Shui**, which focused on arranging physical space to improve health, happiness, and luck.
- **Focus in Tech**: Focuses on how people use or interact with products.

## 2. Fundamentals of Good vs. Poor UX Design

- **Definition of User Experience**: How a person (the user) feels about interacting with or experiencing a product (a good, service, or feature).

### Four Pillars of a Good User Experience

| Pillar | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Usable** | Clear design, structure, and purpose; easy to use | Redesigning glass ketchup bottles into plastic squeezable bottles for better control |
| **Equitable** | Useful and marketable to people with diverse abilities and backgrounds, including those with disabilities | Providing sound alternatives for visually impaired users |
| **Enjoyable** | Creates a positive connection by taking the user's thoughts and feelings into account | Viewing restaurant photos and reviews on takeout apps |
| **Useful** | Solves real problems effectively | A map app successfully guiding a lost user home |

### Business Impact

- Research (e.g., McKinsey & Company) shows that companies prioritizing usability and design perform better financially than competitors because satisfied users return, recommend products, and view the brand positively.

### Poor UX & Norman Doors

- Poor design stems from ignoring user needs rather than personal taste.
- **Norman Doors**: Doors designed with confusing visual cues (e.g., door handles that look like they should be pulled, but must be pushed).
- Icons save screen space and look appealing, but can cause poor UX if their meanings are unclear without clicking.

## 3. Product Development Lifecycle

- **Definition**: The process used to take a product from an idea to reality.

### 5 Stages

1. **Brainstorm**: Active discovery phase generating ideas about user needs and challenges. Features research, interviews, and heavy involvement from UX researchers and writers.
2. **Define**: Narrows focus using brainstorming insights. Outlines goals, product specs, who the product is for, and key features.
3. **Design**: Active creation of visual and functional assets keeping the user top-of-mind.
   - *Storyboards*: Sketches exploring the user's experience.
   - *Wireframes*: Outlines showing layout content.
   - *Prototypes*: Functional models used for testing.
4. **Test**: Evaluates designs based on feedback from potential users and engineers to make interactive and functional improvements.
5. **Launch**: Releases the finished product to the public (e.g., app store listing or live website). Work continues post-launch based on user feedback.

## 4. Roles, Traits, & Cross-Functional Collaboration

- **Common Traits of UX Designers**: Visual intuition, curiosity about human behavior, resourcefulness, and **empathy** (the ability to understand someone else's feelings or thoughts).

### Design Specialist Roles

| Role | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| **Interaction Designers** | How a product functions and its overall user flow |
| **Visual Designers** | Aesthetics, including logos, illustrations, icons, fonts, colors, and layout |
| **Motion Designers** | User movement and smooth transitions between pages or screens |
| **Graphic Designers** | Visual storytelling and appearance, primarily for physical products |

### Non-Design UX Roles

| Role | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| **UX Researchers** | Conduct studies and interviews |
| **UX Writers** | Craft concise, intuitive in-app text and button labels |
| **Production Designers** | Bridge interaction designers and engineers by preparing complete assets (specifications, fonts, spacing) |
| **UX Engineers** | Translate design specs into functional code for web or mobile apps |
| **UX Program Managers** | Ensure project schedules, goals, and communication run smoothly across teams |

### Cross-Functional Teams

- Teams made of varied specialists (e.g., product leads, engineers, designers) collaborating toward a single shared goal: building a usable, equitable, enjoyable, and useful product.

## 5. UX Designer Career Paths & Company Types

### Skill Depth Styles

- **Generalist**: Broad responsibilities across many disciplines; typical for entry-level roles or smaller companies.
- **Specialist**: Deep expertise in one specific area (e.g., interaction design); common in large companies.
- **T-Shaped**: Deep expertise in one specialty (vertical line of the T) supplemented by broad knowledge in related disciplines and soft skills (horizontal line).

### Work Environments

- **Startups / Small Businesses**: Fast-paced environments with tight budgets requiring generalist skillsets; steep learning curve.
- **Freelance**: Self-employed work choosing individual clients, building custom schedules, and managing various brands.
- **Advertising Agencies**: Creative agencies building marketing campaigns for clients; involves varied digital and graphic design tasks.
- **Design Agencies / Studios**: Specialized one-stop shops focusing on design and branding across multiple clients and stages.
- **Large Companies (e.g., Google)**: Focuses on specialized roles on specific projects from start to end with vast expert networks.
